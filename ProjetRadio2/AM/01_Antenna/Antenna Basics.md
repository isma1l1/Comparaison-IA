## Purpose of the Antenna  
  
The **antenna** in an AM radio serves as the **interface between the electromagnetic waves traveling through the air and the radio's electronic circuitry**. Its main functions are:  
  
1. **Capturing Electromagnetic Waves**  
- AM radio signals are transmitted as **amplitude-modulated radio waves**.  
- The antenna converts these propagating electromagnetic waves into a small alternating current (AC) voltage that can be processed by the radio.  
  
2. **Determining Reception Quality**  
- The length, type, and orientation of the antenna strongly affect **signal strength and clarity**.  
- A properly designed antenna increases sensitivity to the desired station and reduces noise from other sources.  
  
3. **Feeding the RF Front-End**  
- The antenna provides the captured signal to the **RF tuning and amplification stage** of the radio.  
- This ensures the weakest signals can still be processed and demodulated effectively.  
  
---  
  
## Key Notes for Beginners  
  
- In a basic AM radio, a **simple long wire** can function as an antenna.  
- The antenna is **passive**: it does not amplify the signal; it only captures it.  
- Orientation and placement matter: vertical or horizontal wires may work differently depending on the transmitted signal and surrounding environment.
