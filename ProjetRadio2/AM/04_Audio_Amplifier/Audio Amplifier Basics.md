

---

## 1. Purpose of the Audio Amplifier

After the envelope detector:

- The signal is **low-frequency audio**  
- Its **amplitude is still small** → not enough to drive a speaker or headphones  

The **audio amplifier** increases the signal amplitude so you can hear it clearly.

---

## 2. What It Does

The audio amplifier:

1. Receives the **output of the envelope detector**  
2. Increases the voltage (or current) of the signal  
3. Provides **enough power to drive a speaker or headphones**  

---

## 3. Why It Comes After the Envelope Detector

- Envelope detector produces audio but **very weak**  
- Direct connection to a speaker would produce **barely audible sound**  
- The amplifier ensures that **the signal is strong enough** for listening


---

## 4. Basic Components of a Simple Audio Amplifier

For a beginner AM radio, a simple **transistor-based amplifier** is enough:

- **Transistor (NPN)** → main amplifying element  
- **Bias resistors** → set transistor operating point  
- **Coupling capacitors** → pass audio signal but block DC  
- **Load resistor or speaker** → converts amplified voltage to sound

---

## 5. Characteristics to Know

- Amplifies **audio frequencies** (20 Hz – 20 kHz)  
- Gain depends on transistor and resistor configuration  
- Can be **single-stage** for headphones or **two-stage** for speaker  

---

## 6. Intuition

- Envelope Detector → “find the music in the RF wave”  
- Audio Amplifier → “make the music loud enough to hear”

---

## 7. Notes

- Simple transistor circuits are enough for **beginner AM radios**  
- You can later replace with **IC-based amplifiers** for better quality  
- Keep connections **short and neat** to reduce noise
